**TAM, SAM, and SOM** are the three layers of market sizing that help you understand how big your opportunity really is — and how much of it you can realistically capture.

> TAM gets investors excited. SAM gets them interested. SOM gets them to write a check.

---

## The Three Layers

| Metric | Full Name | What It Answers | Example (for a project management tool) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | Total Addressable Market | How big is the entire market? | All project management software spending globally (~$6B) |
| **SAM** | Serviceable Available Market | How much of TAM can you actually reach? | Cloud-based PM tools in English-speaking markets (~$2B) |
| **SOM** | Serviceable Obtainable Market | How much can you realistically capture? | PM tools for remote teams in Year 1 (~$50M) |

<details>
<summary>How to calculate each layer (with examples)</summary>

### TAM: Top-Down and Bottom-Up

**Top-Down:** Start with a large industry number from a research firm, then narrow down.
- "The global project management software market is $6B" (Gartner)
- Subtract segments you're not targeting
- Result: Your TAM

**Bottom-Up:** Start with your pricing and multiply by potential customers.
- $20/user/month × 12 months = $240/year per user
- 10 million potential users worldwide
- TAM = $2.4B

**Use both.** If your top-down and bottom-up numbers are wildly different, your assumptions are wrong somewhere.

### SAM: Narrow to What You Can Serve

SAM filters TAM by:
- **Geography:** Are you launching globally or in one country?
- **Customer segment:** Enterprise, SMB, or consumer?
- **Technology:** Does your solution require specific infrastructure?
- **Regulation:** Are there markets you can't legally serve?

**Example:** If your PM tool is English-only and cloud-based, your SAM excludes non-English markets and on-premise-only enterprises.

### SOM: Be Brutally Honest

SOM is your Year 1-3 capture rate. Ask:
- What's your current distribution capability?
- How many customers can your team actually serve?
- What's the realistic conversion rate in your market?
- Who are the incumbents, and how much share can you steal?

**Rule of thumb:** SOM is typically 1-5% of SAM for a new entrant. If you're claiming 20%+ of a market you haven't entered yet, investors will question your math.

</details>

## Common TAM Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong |
|---|---|
| **Claiming the entire industry as TAM** | Not everyone in the industry is your potential customer |
| **Ignoring existing alternatives** | Your real market is people currently using something else, not the total population |
| **Using only top-down numbers** | Industry reports are often inflated; bottom-up is more credible |
| **Confusing TAM with revenue potential** | TAM is the total spending; you'll capture a fraction |
| **Not showing growth** | A $1B market growing at 2%/year is less interesting than a $200M market growing at 30%/year |

## How Investors Evaluate Market Size

| What They Look For | Why |
|---|---|
| **Large TAM ($1B+)** | Big markets can support big outcomes |
| **Growing market** | Growing markets lift all boats |
| **Clear SAM** | Shows you understand your actual target |
| **Realistic SOM** | Shows you understand go-to-market reality |
| **Bottom-up math** | More credible than analyst reports |

> "I'd rather have a small piece of a big market than a big piece of a small market." — Every VC, ever
