A **Flywheel** is a self-reinforcing business model where each element of the business powers the next, creating compounding momentum over time.

> Unlike a funnel that loses energy at every stage, a flywheel stores energy and spins faster the more it runs.

---

## Flywheel vs. Funnel

| Funnel | Flywheel |
|---|---|
| Linear: inputs at top, outputs at bottom | Circular: outputs feed back into inputs |
| Customers are the end result | Customers are the engine |
| Loses energy at every stage | Compounds energy over time |
| Requires constant new investment | Gains momentum organically |
| Customer success happens after the sale | Customer success drives the next sale |

---

## Amazon's Flywheel (The Original)

Jeff Bezos famously sketched Amazon's flywheel on a napkin:

1. **Lower prices** → attract more customers
2. **More customers** → attract more sellers
3. **More sellers** → better selection
4. **Better selection** → improved customer experience
5. **Improved experience** → more traffic
6. **More traffic** → lower cost structure (economies of scale)
7. **Lower cost structure** → even lower prices

> Each loop of the flywheel makes the next loop faster. This is why Amazon can afford to operate on thin margins — the flywheel does the heavy lifting.

<details>
<summary>How to build your own flywheel</summary>

### Step 1: Identify Your Core Value Loop

What is the one thing your product does that creates value for users? That's the center of your flywheel. Everything else spins around it.

**Examples:**
- Netflix: Great content → more viewers → more data → better recommendations → more engagement → more content investment
- LinkedIn: More professionals → more connections → more data → better job matching → more professionals join
- Uber: More drivers → shorter wait times → more riders → more driver income → more drivers

### Step 2: Find the Reinforcing Mechanism

What happens as a result of user action that naturally brings in more users or makes the product better?

| Company | User Action | Reinforcing Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Dropbox | Save a file | Share a link → receiver signs up |
| Slack | Send a message | Teammate joins → more messages → more teammates |
| Airbnb | Book a listing | Host earns money → lists more → more options |

### Step 3: Remove the Friction

Every point of friction in your flywheel is a brake. Identify where users drop off, where the handoff between steps is clumsy, and where the experience breaks down.

**Common friction points:**
- Complex onboarding (users never reach the "aha" moment)
- Poor activation (users sign up but never use the core feature)
- Weak retention (users leave before the flywheel can spin)

### Step 4: Measure the Spin Rate

Track how fast your flywheel is spinning:
- **Time to first value:** How long until a user experiences the core benefit?
- **Referral rate:** What % of users bring in new users?
- **Engagement frequency:** How often do users return?
- **Revenue per user:** Is the value per user growing over time?

## The Four Common Flywheel Models

| Model | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Product-Led** | Product quality drives word of mouth, which drives growth | Slack, Notion, Figma |
| **Content-Led** | User-generated content ranks on search, which drives traffic | Pinterest, TripAdvisor, YouTube |
| **Sales-Led** | Revenue funds more sales capacity, which drives more revenue | Salesforce, HubSpot |
| **Community-Led** | Community engagement attracts new members, who create more engagement | Discord, Reddit, Stack Overflow |

---

## Why Most Startups Get This Wrong

- **They build a funnel, not a flywheel:** Every new customer requires the same amount of acquisition effort. There's no compounding.
- **They ignore the "customer success" spoke:** If customers don't succeed, they don't refer, review, or return. The flywheel breaks.
- **They try to build all four models:** Pick one flywheel and master it before adding complexity.
- **They measure output, not momentum:** Tracking total customers tells you less than tracking how fast each loop cycle completes.
