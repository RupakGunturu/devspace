**Unit Economics** measures the revenue and cost associated with a single unit of your business — usually one customer. If your unit economics don't work, your business doesn't work.

> You can lose money at scale. But if you lose money on every customer, scaling just means losing money faster.

---

## The Core Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue per unit** | Total revenue ÷ Number of units | Higher is better |
| **Cost per unit** | Total variable costs ÷ Number of units | Lower is better |
| **Gross margin** | (Revenue - COGS) ÷ Revenue | > 60% for SaaS, > 30% for physical products |
| **Contribution margin** | (Revenue - Variable costs) ÷ Revenue | Positive = each unit is profitable |

<details>
<summary>The difference between gross margin and contribution margin</summary>

### Gross Margin

Gross margin only considers the direct cost of delivering your product (COGS — Cost of Goods Sold). For SaaS, this includes:
- Hosting/cloud costs
- Payment processing fees
- Support costs (if tied to usage)

**SaaS gross margins are typically 70-85%.** If yours is below 60%, your hosting or delivery costs are too high.

### Contribution Margin

Contribution margin goes further — it includes ALL variable costs, not just COGS:
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- Sales commissions
- Onboarding costs
- Support costs
- Infrastructure costs

**Contribution margin tells you the real profit per unit after everything variable is paid.** If contribution margin is positive, each new customer adds to your bottom line. If negative, each new customer costs you money.

### Why This Matters

A SaaS company with 80% gross margin but 40% contribution margin means:
- The product is cheap to deliver (good)
- But acquiring and supporting customers is expensive (bad)
- The real profit per customer is 40%, not 80%

</details>

## Unit Economics by Business Model

| Model | Revenue | Key Costs | Healthy Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SaaS** | Monthly subscription | Hosting, support, CAC | > 70% gross, > 30% contribution |
| **Marketplace** | Transaction fee | Payment processing, support | > 50% gross |
| **E-commerce** | Product sale | COGS, shipping, returns | > 40% gross |
| **Ad-supported** | CPM/CPC | Content creation, hosting | > 60% gross |
| **Hardware** | Product sale | Manufacturing, shipping | > 30% gross |

## The Unit Economics Formula

```
Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) - Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) = Profit per Customer
```

If this number is negative, you're losing money on every customer. If positive, you have a business.

### Adding Time to the Equation

```
Payback Period = CAC ÷ Monthly Gross Margin per Customer
```

If it takes 18 months to recover your CAC but customers only stay 12 months, you lose money on every customer — even if the LTV:CAC ratio looks good on paper.

---

## Red Flags in Unit Economics

| Red Flag | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Negative gross margin | You're losing money on every sale before even counting acquisition |
| CAC > LTV | You pay more to acquire a customer than they'll ever pay you |
| Payback period > customer lifetime | You never recoup your acquisition cost |
| Gross margin declining over time | Costs are growing faster than revenue |
| High variable costs that should be fixed | Your cost structure doesn't scale efficiently |
