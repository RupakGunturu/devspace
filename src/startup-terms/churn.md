**Churn** is the rate at which customers stop using your product. It's the silent killer of growth — you can acquire all the new users you want, but if they leave faster than they arrive, you're filling a leaky bucket.

> A product with 5% monthly churn loses 46% of its customers every year. That means you need to replace nearly half your user base just to stay flat.

---

## The Churn Math

| Metric | Formula | What It Tells You |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly churn rate** | Customers lost ÷ Customers at start of month | How fast you're losing users |
| **Revenue churn** | Revenue lost ÷ Revenue at start of month | How fast you're losing money |
| **Net revenue churn** | (Revenue lost - Expansion revenue) ÷ Revenue at start | Whether existing customers are growing faster than you're losing them |
| **Retention rate** | 1 - Churn rate | The inverse — what % of users stay |

<details>
<summary>Why net negative churn is the holy grail</summary>

### The Magic of Net Negative Churn

When your existing customers spend more over time (through upsells, cross-sells, or usage growth) than you lose from churn, you achieve **net negative churn**. This means:

- You could stop acquiring new customers entirely
- And your revenue would **still grow**

This is the most powerful state a SaaS business can reach. It means your existing customer base is your growth engine.

**How to achieve it:**
1. **Usage-based pricing:** Customers pay more as they use more (AWS, Twilio)
2. **Tiered plans:** As customers grow, they upgrade to higher tiers
3. **Add-on products:** Sell complementary products to existing customers
4. **Excellent customer success:** Proactively help customers get more value

### The Cohort Analysis

Don't look at churn in aggregate — look at **cohorts** (groups of users who signed up in the same period):

| Cohort | Month 1 | Month 3 | Month 6 | Month 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | 100% | 65% | 45% | 30% |
| February | 100% | 70% | 52% | 38% |
| March | 100% | 75% | 58% | 45% |

If later cohorts retain better than earlier ones, your product is improving. If retention is getting worse, something is broken.

## The Types of Churn

| Type | What It Is | How to Measure |
|---|---|---|
| **Voluntary** | User actively decides to leave | Exit surveys, cancellation reasons |
| **Involuntary** | Payment fails, account expires | Failed payment tracking |
| **Competitive** | User switches to a competitor | Win/loss analysis |
| **Gradual** | User slowly uses product less | Usage frequency trends |
| **Instant** | User signs up, never comes back | Day 1, Day 7 retention |

---

## Reducing Churn: The Playbook

| Strategy | Impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Improve onboarding** | Users who reach the "aha" moment in day 1 retain 2-3x better | Medium |
| **Proactive customer success** | Reach out before users churn, not after | High |
| **Usage-based triggers** | Alert when usage drops — "We noticed you haven't logged in for a while" | Low |
| **Exit interviews** | Learn why people leave — the reasons are usually fixable | Low |
| **Annual plans** | Reduces monthly churn by locking in commitment | Low |
| **Build switching costs** | Make it painful to leave (data, integrations, workflows) | Medium |

## The Churn Benchmark

| Industry | Good Churn | Average Churn | Bad Churn |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B SaaS (Enterprise) | < 1% monthly | 1-2% | > 3% |
| B2B SaaS (SMB) | < 3% monthly | 3-5% | > 7% |
| B2C SaaS | < 5% monthly | 5-8% | > 10% |
| E-commerce (subscriptions) | < 5% monthly | 5-10% | > 15% |

> These are **monthly** rates. Compounded annually, even "good" churn means replacing a significant portion of your base every year.

---

## The Uncomfortable Truth

Most startups obsess over acquisition (new users) and ignore retention (keeping users). But increasing retention by 5% can increase profits by 25-95% (Bain & Company research). The cheapest user to acquire is the one you already have.
