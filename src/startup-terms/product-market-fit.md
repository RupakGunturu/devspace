**Product-Market Fit (PMF)** is the point where your product satisfies a strong market demand — people aren't just trying it, they're sticking with it and telling others.

> Marc Andreessen: "Product-market fit means being in a good market with a product that can satisfy that market."

---

## How Do You Know You Have PMF?

There's no single metric, but there are strong signals:

| Signal | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Organic growth** | Users are finding you without paid ads — word of mouth, search, social |
| **Retention** | Users come back without being prompted — weekly active usage stays flat or grows |
| **Sean Ellis test** | >40% of surveyed users say they'd be "very disappointed" if your product disappeared |
| **Engagement depth** | Users aren't just signing up — they're using core features repeatedly |
| **Sales cycle shortens** | New customers convert faster because the value proposition is clear |

<details>
<summary>The uncomfortable truth about PMF</summary>

### PMF Is Not a Finish Line

Finding PMF doesn't mean you're done. It means you've found a starting point. Markets shift, competitors enter, and user expectations evolve. PMF is something you have to **maintain**, not just achieve.

### Before PMF: Do Things That Don't Scale

Paul Graham's famous advice applies directly here. Before PMF, your job isn't to optimize funnels or build automation — it's to **talk to users**, understand their pain, and manually deliver value. The Airbnb founders were literally photographing apartments themselves before PMF.

### The "Superpower" Test

If your product is a superpower for even 10 users, you might have PMF. If nobody would notice if you shut down tomorrow, you don't. Quality of devotion matters more than quantity of users at this stage.

</details>

## The PMF Journey

| Stage | Focus | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-PMF** | Learning, iterating, talking to users | Building the wrong thing |
| **PMF Signal** | Retention curves flatten, organic growth appears | Scaling too early |
| **Post-PMF** | Growth engines, hiring, scaling infrastructure | Losing what made you special |

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Scaling before PMF:** Pouring money into ads when retention is still terrible. You're just filling a leaky bucket.
- **Vanity metrics:** Celebrating sign-ups while ignoring the fact that 90% of users never come back.
- **Copying competitors:** Building features because a competitor has them, instead of solving your users' specific pain.
- **Ignoring qualitative feedback:** The numbers tell you *what* is happening. Talking to users tells you *why*.
