**Virality** is when your product spreads through users — each user brings in more users, creating exponential growth without proportional spending on acquisition.

> Virality isn't luck. It's engineering the conditions for organic sharing.

---

## The Virality Formula

| Metric | Formula | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **K-factor** | Invites sent × Conversion rate of invites | If K > 1, growth is exponential |
| **Viral cycle time** | Time between when a user sends an invite and when the recipient converts | Shorter = faster growth |

**Example:**
- Each user sends 3 invites
- 20% of invites convert
- K-factor = 3 × 0.2 = 0.6 (not viral — each generation shrinks)
- K-factor = 5 × 0.3 = 1.5 (viral — each generation grows by 50%)

<summary>The truth about virality most founders miss</summary>

### Virality Is Rare — And That's Okay

True viral growth (K > 1) is extremely rare. Most successful products don't go viral. They build sustainable, predictable growth through a combination of channels.

**Don't build your strategy around virality.** Build a great product that happens to be shareable. If virality comes, it's a bonus.

### The Two Types of Virality

**Organic virality:** Users share because the product is genuinely useful or fun.
- Examples: WhatsApp ("everyone I know is on it"), TikTok ("this video is amazing")
- Requirement: The product must create something worth sharing

**Incentivized virality:** Users share because you reward them.
- Examples: Dropbox ("invite friends, get storage"), Uber ("give $10, get $10")
- Risk: Incentives attract low-quality users who only care about the reward

### The Sharing Loop

For virality to work, three things must happen:

1. **User creates shareable content** — A video, a project, a link, a score
2. **User shares it** — One click, no friction
3. **Recipient sees value and converts** — They understand what the product does from the shared content

If any of these steps break, the loop fails.

### Why Most "Viral" Products Aren't Actually Viral

- **Social media amplification ≠ virality:** A product that gets talked about on Twitter might get a spike in signups, but if those users don't invite others, it's just marketing, not virality.
- **Network effects ≠ virality:** LinkedIn has network effects (more users = more valuable), but it's not viral (users don't bring in other users through the product).
- **Word of mouth ≠ virality:** People recommending your product verbally is powerful but not viral in the technical sense.

## The 4 Types of Viral Products

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Communication** | Product is the communication channel | WhatsApp, Slack, Zoom |
| **Content** | Users create content that attracts others | TikTok, YouTube, Instagram |
| **Collaboration** | Product requires multiple users | Figma, Google Docs, Notion |
| **Status** | Users share for social proof | Wordle scores, Spotify Wrapped, fitness apps |

## Engineering Shareability

| Factor | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| **One-click sharing** | "Share" button that auto-fills the message | "Copy this link and paste it yourself" |
| **Built-in content** | The shared item looks great on its own | Shared link goes to a login wall |
| **Social proof** | "500 people are using this" | No indication of popularity |
| **Timing** | Share prompt after a moment of delight | Share prompt on first visit |
| **Incentive alignment** | Both sharer and recipient benefit | Only the sharer benefits |

## Viral Marketing vs. Viral Product

| Viral Marketing | Viral Product |
|---|---|
| Marketing campaign spreads quickly | The product itself spreads through use |
| Short-lived (days/weeks) | Long-lived (built into the product) |
| Requires constant creativity | Compounds over time |
| Example: Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" | Example: Dropbox referral program |
| Doesn't require a great product | Requires a great product |

> The best viral marketing is a product so good that users can't help but share it.

---

## When Virality Fails

- **The product isn't shareable:** If there's nothing to share, there's no loop.
- **The recipient doesn't understand:** If the shared content doesn't explain the product, recipients won't convert.
- **The incentive is wrong:** Paying users to invite friends attracts freeloaders, not customers.
- **Privacy concerns:** Users won't share things that expose their data or behavior.
- **The product has no network effect:** Even if users invite others, the product doesn't get better with more users.
