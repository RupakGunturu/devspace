**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** is what you spend to get one new customer. **LTV (Lifetime Value)** is how much revenue that customer generates over their entire relationship with you.

> If it costs you $100 to acquire a customer who only gives you $50 back, you don't have a business — you have a hobby with a budget.

---

## The Core Ratio

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC** | Total sales & marketing spend ÷ New customers acquired | Lower is better |
| **LTV** | Average revenue per customer × Average customer lifespan | Higher is better |
| **LTV:CAC Ratio** | LTV ÷ CAC | Should be **3:1 or higher** |

<details>
<summary>Why the 3:1 ratio matters</summary>

### The Math Behind the Magic

If your LTV:CAC ratio is:
- **< 1:1** — You're losing money on every customer. Stop spending immediately.
- **1:1** — Breaking even. No profit, no growth capital.
- **2:1** — Surviving, but barely. One bad quarter could sink you.
- **3:1** — Healthy. You have margin for error and reinvestment.
- **5:1+** — You might be under-investing in growth. You could afford to spend more on acquisition.

### The Hidden Costs

Most CAC calculations only count ad spend. But real CAC includes:
- Marketing team salaries
- Content creation costs
- Sales team compensation
- Tools and software (CRM, analytics, email)
- Time spent on partnerships and outreach

Similarly, LTV often ignores refunds, support costs, and the time value of money. Calculate both conservatively.

### Payback Period Matters Too

Even with a great LTV:CAC ratio, if your payback period is 18 months, you need significant cash flow to sustain growth. A SaaS company with 3-month payback can grow much faster than one with 12-month payback, even at the same ratio.

</details>

## How to Improve Your Ratio

| Lever | Action | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Increase LTV** | Upsell, cross-sell, improve retention | Customers stay longer and spend more |
| **Decrease CAC** | Improve conversion rates, leverage organic channels | Each customer costs less to acquire |
| **Shorten payback** | Move to annual plans, reduce onboarding friction | Cash comes back faster |
| **Focus on high-LTV segments** | Target customers who naturally stick around longer | Better ratio from day one |

## CAC by Channel

Not all channels cost the same. Track CAC separately for each:

| Channel | Typical CAC Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Organic / SEO | Low (time-heavy) | Slow to start, compounds over time |
| Content marketing | Low-Medium | Requires consistent output |
| Paid ads (Google/Meta) | Medium-High | Fast, but costs rise with competition |
| Sales team | High | Best for enterprise/B2B |
| Referrals | Very low | Best when product is already great |

The goal isn't to minimize CAC across the board — it's to find the channels where CAC is low *relative to the LTV of customers from that channel*.
