**Retention** is the ability to keep users engaged with your product over time. It's the most important metric in any business — because without retention, nothing else matters.

> You can't pour water into a bucket with a hole in it. Retention is plugging the hole before you turn on the faucet.

---

## The Retention Metrics

| Metric | Formula | What It Tells You |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 1 retention** | % of users who return the next day | Did they find initial value? |
| **Day 7 retention** | % of users who return after a week | Did they form a habit? |
| **Day 30 retention** | % of users who return after a month | Is this product stick? |
| **Churn rate** | % of users who leave per period | How fast you're losing users |
| **NPS** | Net Promoter Score | Would users recommend you? |

<details>
<summary>The retention curve — the most important chart in your business</summary>

### What the Curve Looks Like

Plot the % of users who remain active over time. A healthy retention curve:

1. **Drops sharply** in the first few days (normal — not everyone is your target user)
2. **Flattens** at some point (these are your retained users)
3. **Stays flat** over months (product-market fit indicator)

An unhealthy curve:
1. Drops sharply
2. **Keeps dropping** — never flattens
3. Approaches zero

**If your curve never flattens, you don't have product-market fit.** No amount of growth spending will fix this.

### The Activation Threshold

The key to retention is **activation** — the moment a user experiences the core value of your product. This is often called the "aha moment."

**Examples of activation moments:**
- Facebook: "Added 7 friends in 10 days"
- Slack: "Sent 2,000 team messages"
- Dropbox: "Put first file in Dropbox"
- Twitter: "Followed 30 people"

Find YOUR activation moment. Then optimize everything to get users there faster.

### The Retention Levers

| Lever | Action | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Activation** | Get users to the "aha" moment faster | High — users who activate retain 2-5x better |
| **Habit formation** | Build triggers that bring users back | High — daily use creates lock-in |
| **Value deepening** | Help users discover more value over time | Medium — reduces gradual churn |
| **Social hooks** | Create dependencies on other users | High — makes leaving costly |
| **Notification strategy** | Smart, relevant reminders (not spam) | Medium — re-engages dormant users |

## The Retention Framework: HEART

| Letter | Metric | What to Measure |
|---|---|---|
| **H** | Happiness | NPS, satisfaction surveys |
| **E** | Engagement | Frequency, depth, duration of use |
| **A** | Adoption | % of users who try new features |
| **R** | Retention | % of users who return over time |
| **T** | Task success | Can users accomplish their goals? |

## Retention by Business Type

| Type | What Retention Looks Like | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily use (Slack, Gmail)** | Users come back every day | D1 > 60%, D30 > 40% |
| **Weekly use (Notion, Figma)** | Users come back weekly | D1 > 50%, D30 > 30% |
| **Monthly use (billing tools)** | Users interact monthly | M1 > 70%, M3 > 50% |
| **Seasonal (travel, tax)** | Users return at predictable intervals | Year-over-year retention > 40% |

## Common Retention Mistakes

- **Optimizing acquisition over retention:** Getting new users is pointless if they all leave.
- **Treating all users the same:** New users need onboarding; power users need advanced features.
- **Ignoring cohort analysis:** Aggregate retention hides whether you're improving or getting worse.
- **Confusing activity with retention:** Users opening emails isn't retention. Users getting value is.
- **Not measuring activation:** If you don't know what triggers retention, you can't optimize for it.
