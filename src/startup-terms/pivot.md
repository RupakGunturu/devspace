A **Pivot** is a fundamental change in business direction — not just tweaking a feature, but shifting the core strategy, target audience, or value proposition.

> A pivot isn't a failure. It's a recognition that what you learned doesn't match what you assumed, and you're smart enough to change course.

---

## When to Pivot vs. When to Persevere

| Pivot Signals | Persevere Signals |
|---|---|
| Retention is declining despite improvements | Retention is flat or slowly improving |
| You've talked to 50+ users and the pain point doesn't resonate | Users consistently say "I'd be lost without this" |
| Your CAC keeps rising with diminishing returns | Organic growth is steady, even if slow |
| The market is shrinking or being commoditized | The market is growing |
| You're building features nobody asks for | Users are requesting features you haven't built yet |

## The 10 Types of Pivot (from Eric Ries)

| # | Pivot Type | What Changes | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Zoom-in** | A single feature becomes the whole product | Instagram started as Burbn (a check-in app) |
| 2 | **Zoom-out** | Your product becomes a feature of a larger offering | Slack started as a gaming tool internal chat |
| 3 | **Customer segment** | Same product, different audience | YouTube was a dating site before becoming video for everyone |
| 4 | **Customer need** | Same audience, different problem |pivot | PayPal started on Palm Pilots before moving to web |
| 5 | **Platform** | Shift from app to platform or vice versa | Facebook opened its platform to third-party apps |
| 6 | **Business model** | Change how you make money | Netflix pivoted from DVD-by-mail to streaming |
| 7 | **Channel** | Change how you reach customers | Many SaaS tools pivoted from sales-led to product-led |
| 8 | **Technology** | Same solution, different tech stack | Most AI startups pivoted from their original approach |
| 9 | **Value capture** | Change what part of the value chain you own | Amazon Web Services emerged from Amazon's internal infrastructure |
| 10 | **Engine of growth** | Change your growth mechanism | Dropbox pivoted from paid ads to referral loops |

<details>
<summary>How to execute a pivot without dying</summary>

### 1. Anchor on What You've Learned

A pivot without learning is just random direction changes. Write down:
- What assumption was wrong?
- What did users actually tell you?
- What's the one thing that IS working?

### 2. Keep the Core (If Possible)

The best pivots preserve what's working and change what isn't. Instagram kept the photo-sharing UX from Burbn. YouTube kept the video hosting technology. Find the kernel of value in your current product.

### 3. Talk to Users Before You Build

Before pivoting, validate the new direction with the same rigor you should have used at the start:
- Would 10 users pay for this?
- Is this a growing market?
- Can you reach these customers efficiently?

### 4. Set a Timebox

Give yourself 2-4 weeks to validate the new direction before committing fully. If you can't get signal in that time, the pivot might be wrong too.

### 5. Communicate Clearly

Your team, investors, and existing users deserve to know why you're changing direction. Transparency builds trust even when the news isn't exciting.

</details>

## Famous Pivots

| Company | Before | After | What They Learned |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | Burbn (check-in app) | Photo sharing | Users only used the photo feature |
| YouTube | Video dating site | Video sharing platform | People wanted to share all videos, not just dating profiles |
| Slack | Gaming tool (Glitch) | Team communication | The internal chat tool was more valuable than the game |
| Twitter | Odeo (podcast platform) | Microblogging | The SMS status feature was the real product |
| Shopify | Online snowboard store | E-commerce platform | The store-building tool was more valuable than the boards |
